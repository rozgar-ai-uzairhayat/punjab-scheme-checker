How Punjab Scheme Checker works: a step-by-step guide to checking your welfare eligibility

If you've ever wondered whether you qualify for a Punjab government welfare scheme — Benazir Kafaalat, Sehat Card Plus, the Ehsaas Scholarship, or the Nigahban Senior Citizen Card — you've probably run into the same problem everyone else does: the official rules are scattered across different websites, written in dense language, and hard to check without visiting an office in person.

Punjab Scheme Checker was built to solve exactly that. This guide explains how the tool works, what happens to your answers, and how to read your results.

What Punjab Scheme Checker actually does

Punjab Scheme Checker is a free, independent tool that asks you a handful of simple questions — your household income, family size, age, employment status, and a couple of yes/no questions — and instantly shows you which major Punjab welfare schemes you may qualify for.

It is not a government website, and it doesn't submit anything on your behalf. Think of it as a smart first step: it tells you where you stand and exactly where to go next, before you spend time visiting an office or calling a helpline.

Step-by-step: how to use it

Step 1: Answer questions about your household

You'll be asked for your monthly household income and the number of people in your household. This information decides which income-based schemes apply to you.

Step 2: Answer questions about yourself

Next, you'll enter your age and current employment status. Some schemes — like the senior citizen card — depend entirely on age.

Step 3: Select anything else that applies

The last step asks about a couple of specific situations: whether you're currently enrolled in an on-campus undergraduate degree, and whether your household is already registered with BISP. These affect scholarship and BISP-linked program eligibility.

Step 4: Get your results, split into two clear groups

This is the part that makes Punjab Scheme Checker different from a generic quiz. Your results are split into two honest categories:

  • Direct verdict schemes — for programs with clear, stable rules (like Punjab Sehat Card Plus, which is now open to every Punjab resident with a CNIC), the tool gives you a straight "Eligible" or "Not eligible yet" answer, with the reason explained in plain language.
  • Worth checking officially — for programs like Benazir Kafaalat, real eligibility depends on a household survey your income alone can't predict. Instead of guessing, the tool tells you honestly that this needs an official check, and gives you the exact next step (like the 8171 SMS service or your nearest BISP office).

Why the tool refuses to guess on some schemes

You might notice Benazir Kafaalat and Benazir Nashonuma never get a simple "yes" or "no." That's intentional, not a limitation of the tool.

Real Kafaalat eligibility is calculated using a Proxy Means Test (PMT) score, built from a household survey that looks at land ownership, vehicle ownership, utility bills, and other assets — not the income number you'd type into a form. No online tool can calculate that score without the actual survey. Rather than give you a confident-sounding wrong answer, Punjab Scheme Checker tells you plainly when a scheme needs an official check instead.

Is my data safe?

Your answers are used only to calculate your result on the spot. Nothing is stored, sold, or shared. You don't need to create an account or provide your CNIC number to use the tool.

Is this an official government tool?

No. Punjab Scheme Checker is an independent project, not affiliated with the Government of Punjab or the Government of Pakistan. It's built using publicly available scheme rules to help people get a quick, honest starting point. Always confirm final eligibility with the official scheme office before applying or relying on any result.

Frequently asked questions

Is Punjab Scheme Checker free to use?

Yes, completely free, with no account or sign-up required.

Does it work for schemes outside Punjab?

No, currently it covers Punjab-specific and federal schemes available to Punjab residents only.

How accurate is the result?

For schemes with clear public rules (like Sehat Card Plus), the result is directly calculated and accurate. For schemes based on household surveys (like Kafaalat), the tool tells you it can't calculate a final answer and points you to the correct official check instead.
